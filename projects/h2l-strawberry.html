My focus
Software, hardware, and field support

This project used H2L's BodySharing technology to let people experience strawberry harvesting from a remote location. A person operated a robot at the farm through body movements, while camera feedback helped them observe the task.

FirstVR, a device worn on the arm, captures movement and force-related information for remote robot control. The system connects a person's actions to a robot working elsewhere, allowing participation in agricultural activities without traveling to the farm.

System description paraphrased from H2L's official product overview. This demonstration involves human-operated teleoperation.

My contribution

I worked with the existing development team to improve the system across software and hardware, and supported its use in the field. The robot platform was RT Corporation's Sciurus17; my contributions focused on adapting and improving the existing system.

  • ROS-based control software: Modified and improved the robot's existing control software.
  • Finger hardware: Refined the 3D models of finger components and fabricated the updated parts.
  • Motion teaching: Improved AI algorithms used to teach robot motions from human movements.
  • On-site demonstrations: Operated the system and supported the remote harvesting experience in the field.
  • Field repairs: Diagnosed and repaired hardware problems during on-site operation.

Connecting software with physical systems

The work required moving between robot control, mechanical parts, and practical operation. Software improvements had to work with the physical robot, while hardware changes had to support the intended motions and interactions.

This experience strengthened my interest in reliable manipulation and human–robot interfaces: building a working demonstration requires both motion-control software and hands-on understanding of the hardware.

Credits & sources
