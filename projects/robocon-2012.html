02

Problem & motivation

The theme of the 2012 KOSEN Robocon competition was Best Pet. For the first time in its history, teams had to compete without conventional controllers. Students and their pet-inspired robots needed to coordinate directly.

Each team consisted of a robot and a human partner. The opposing team scattered balls with a break shot. The human partner then collected balls from the field and passed them to the robot, which attempted to place them into nine goals.

The challenge combined human–robot cooperation with reliable mechanical execution under competition conditions.

Source: Official 2012 KOSEN Robocon competition description ↗

03

My contribution

I was responsible for the robot's ball-launching mechanism. My work included:

  1. Design: Developing the mechanical launching mechanism.
  2. Fabrication: Building the physical mechanism.
  3. Experiments: Testing and evaluating its performance.
  4. Iteration: Refining the mechanism based on experimental observations.

These were my contributions within a broader team effort; the robot's other subsystems were developed collaboratively.

04

Results

Our robot completed all nine goals in every match, achieving a 100% goal-completion record (9/9 per match). Our team won the 2012 KOSEN Robocon National Championship.

Goal completion refers to completing all nine target goals, not to every individual launch succeeding on its first attempt.

Championship: Official competition results ↗.

05

Limitations & future work

Despite the successful goal completion, the robot's walking speed could have been improved. Faster locomotion might have reduced the time required to complete each task. Future work would focus on increasing speed while preserving the reliability of the launching mechanism.
