Ball-launching mechanism

Japan’s nationally televised KOSEN Robocon is co-organized by NHK and supported by Japan’s education ministry. In 2012, 124 teams entered regional qualifiers, and 25 advanced to the national final.

In Best Pet, each team paired a controller-free robot with a human runner to score balls in nine goals. The runner collected and handed every ball to the robot; the robot could not pick up balls itself and had to show joy when receiving one. Wanko Kyodai did this by rotating its face 360 degrees.

Our robot: Wanko Kyodai

Our team built a walking robot to receive balls from its human partner and place them into the goals. The challenge was to combine locomotion, human–robot coordination, and reliable mechanical execution.

My contribution

Launcher design

I designed and built the ball-launching mechanism.

  • Lightweight: Just 4 kg (8.8 lb), helping the robot move quickly.
  • Reliable: Easy to load; no balls spilled from walking vibrations, and the mechanism remained intact through all nine matches.
  • Accurate: Scored all nine goals on the first attempt in all four regional and five national matches. It was the only launcher in the competition to do so.

Results

Wanko Kyodai won the 2012 national championship.
